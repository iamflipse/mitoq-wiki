MitoQ & Neuronal Function

Source: §9.2 of the paper

Mitochondria in neurodegeneration

  • Mitochondrial dysfunction is commonly observed in aged brains and in Alzheimer's, Parkinson's, Huntington's, and ALS — central to their pathology.
  • Aging → mitochondrial damage → excessive oxidative stress → mtDNA mutations + energy crisis (↓ATP) → impaired neurotransmitter synthesis, synaptic transmission, and membrane potential.

Peroxynitrite and oxidative damage

  • ONOO⁻ (from NO + superoxide) initiates oxidative modifications of DNA, proteins, and lipids; its byproducts (e.g., 3-nitrotyrosine) are oxidative-stress markers in neurodegeneration.
  • 5 weeks of MitoQ in aged rat brains: ↓protein and lipid oxidation (nitrotyrosine), restored ETC complex I & IV activity (↑NADPH oxidase and cytochrome c activities), improved mitochondrial membrane potential + ATP production.
  • MitoQ's anti-aging brain effects attributed to ONOO⁻ scavenging.

Cognitive function

  • Spatial and object-recognition memory in aged mice: MitoQ alleviated age-related deterioration by restoring ATP production capacity in synaptic mitochondria (Fig. 2C).
  • AD transgenic mice: MitoQ administration extended lifespan — therapeutic potential in neurodegenerative conditions.
