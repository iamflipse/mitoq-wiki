MitoQ: Chemistry & Mitochondrial Targeting

Source: §8 of the paper

From CoQ10 to MitoQ

  • CoQ10 (coenzyme Q10): endogenous lipid-soluble quinone in the inner mitochondrial membrane; mobile electron carrier (complexes I/II → III) + antioxidant (reduced ubiquinol form scavenges ROS, regenerates vitamin E).
  • Clinical evidence: meta-analysis shows CoQ10 improves endothelial function (FMD) in CVD patients; head-to-head CoQ10 vs MitoQ in middle-aged men: both reduced skeletal-muscle mitochondrial H₂O₂ emission without changing respiratory capacity or mitochondrial content.
  • Pharmacokinetic limits of CoQ10: lipophilic, poorly water-soluble, low oral bioavailability; distributes broadly across tissues rather than accumulating selectively in mitochondria → high interindividual variability.

MitoQ design

  • MitoQ = ubiquinone moiety + lipophilic triphenylphosphonium (TPP⁺) cation, linked via an alkyl chain (Fig. 2A).
  • MitoQ is not simply a "targeted CoQ10" — the TPP⁺ conjugation changes its physicochemical behavior; it no longer functions as a physiological electron carrier. Its role is selective mitochondrial accumulation + local redox modulation.
  • Targeting mechanism: TPP⁺ is a positively charged phosphorus with three hydrophobic phenyl groups; it crosses membranes efficiently and accumulates in the negatively charged mitochondrial compartment — TPP⁺ concentration rises ~10-fold per 60 mV of membrane potential.
  • Accumulation: ~5–10× in cytosol, up to ~1000× (100–500-fold) in the mitochondrial matrix (Fig. 2B).
  • In-mitochondria redox cycle: once inside, the ubiquinone moiety is reduced to ubiquinol (by complex II); ubiquinol scavenges mtROS → H₂O₂ → re-oxidized to ubiquinone → continuous recycling and retention in the matrix.

Downstream effects beyond direct scavenging

  • MitoQ inhibits NF-κB signaling in human umbilical vein endothelial cells → ↓transcriptional priming of NLRP3 and proinflammatory cytokines (S. Chen 2019).
  • Normalizes excessive autophagy, maintains mitochondrial integrity, indirectly suppresses caspase-1 activation and IL-1β maturation.
  • Net effect: attenuates the vicious ROS↔mitochondrial-homeostasis cycle (ETC function, dynamics, Ca²⁺ signaling) and NLRP3 inflammasome activation.
  • Translationally, MitoQ is the most advanced mitochondria-targeted antioxidant: demonstrated oral bioavailability + human trial data, commercially available (vs MitoTEMPO, SkQ1 — mostly preclinical tools).
