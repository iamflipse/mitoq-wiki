# MitoQ & Lower Urinary Tract Dysfunction (LUTS)

*Source: [§9.4 of the paper](../sources/mitoq-fulltext.md)*

## Why LUTS is an aging/metabolic-syndrome problem

- Up to **half of older adults** experience LUTS (urgency, incontinence, nocturia, frequency, hesitancy, incomplete emptying) — with big impacts on sleep, depression, social isolation, falls, nursing-home admission.
- **70% of hospitalized patients rate incontinence equal to or worse than death.**
- Current pharmacotherapy (antimuscarinics, β3-agonists) is palliative, modestly effective, poorly tolerated, and raises cognitive-impairment risk in the elderly.
- **Metabolic syndrome (MetS)** — glucose intolerance, obesity, hypertension, dyslipidemia ("accelerated aging") — is a major LUTS risk factor; MetS and aging share the same hallmarks: **inflammation, mitochondrial dysfunction, deregulated nutrient sensing** (Fig. 4), plus ischemia and endothelial dysfunction.

## Inflammation in LUTS

- In OAB patients: **↑CRP, NGF, MCP-1** correlate with age; pathway analysis links **immune/inflammatory pathways** to OAB; urine proteomics in LUTS women shows enriched **inflammation, dysregulated ion transport, and immune response** pathways.
- In rodents: **NLRP3 inflammasome upregulates with age in urothelial cells** → inflammation, fibrosis, urothelial remodeling, detrusor dysfunction. NLRP3 activation triggers in the bladder: toxic urinary contents, hypoxia/reperfusion, pressure, repetitive stretching.

## Oxidative stress & ischemia in LUTS

- ROS damages detrusor myocytes, urothelium, vasculature, and bladder innervation → cell death.
- Bladder **ischemia/hypoxia** (pelvic atherosclerosis) → ROS + pelvic vascular insufficiency → LUTS in older adults; repeated ischemia/reperfusion → denervation + fibrosis.
- In aged-rat models, **ROS in bladder epithelium** disrupts mitochondrial bioenergetics → urothelial barrier/sensory dysfunction.
- Urinary metabolites of mitochondrial dysfunction/oxidative stress/ketosis are **elevated in older female MetS patients with OAB** — potential OAB diagnostic/severity biomarkers.

## The MitoQ hypothesis + trial

- Rationale: MitoQ scavenges mtROS, **inhibits NLRP3 inflammasome**, ↑mitophagy of dysfunctional mitochondria, and improves NO availability — all affecting detrusor, nerves, urothelium, and vasculature.
- **Pilot/feasibility trial (NCT06351683):** 40 mg/day MitoQ vs placebo on **urinary urgency and other LUTS in women ≥50 with MetS** (PI: Al-Naggar).
- Proposed next targets: LUTS from **BPH, urethral stricture/bladder outlet obstruction**, where ischemia/reperfusion-driven oxidative stress is implicated.
