# MitoQ & Skeletal Muscle Function

*Source: [§9.3 of the paper](../sources/mitoq-fulltext.md)*

## Sarcopenia background

- Sarcopenia = age-related progressive loss of muscle mass/strength → declines in physical function, mobility, independence.
- Mechanisms: loss of motor units, ↓anabolic hormones/protein synthesis, ↑connective tissue.
- Excessive **ROS in high-oxygen-demand tissue** (skeletal muscle) is a key sarcopenia factor; antioxidant strategies may mitigate oxidative damage in the aged.
- Other mitochondria-targeted antioxidants (**SkQ, SS-31, XJB-5/131**) show effects in aged rodent models; MitoQ's results are **inconsistent** — the key nuance of this section.

## Preclinical: dose- and duration-dependent

- **Sakellariou et al.:** 15 weeks × 100 µM MitoQ **failed** to reduce age-related oxidative damage or reverse muscle mass/functional decline in ~28-month-old mice.
- **Murray et al. (2026):** 4 weeks × 250 µM MitoQ in 27-month-old mice **reduced mtROS and inflammation** and **enhanced physical performance**.
- Takeaway: MitoQ effects in aged skeletal muscle **depend on dosage and treatment duration** — 250 µM/4 weeks works where 100 µM/15 weeks did not.

## Clinical

- 6 weeks × 20 mg/day in **middle-aged humans**: ↓mtROS with **no change** in mitochondrial respiration/content/biogenesis proteins.
- **Murray et al.** clinical trial (20 mg/day, 6 weeks, older adults): no overall physical-function gain, but **subgroup analysis: only participants >70 years** showed increased **peak leg-extension power and grip strength** — suggests benefit tracks baseline aging severity.
- 12 weeks of MitoQ in older adults: ↓mitochondrial H₂O₂ emission; no change in respiration, ADP sensitivity, redox-sensitive kinase signaling, or exercise-induced mitochondrial/antioxidant gene expression.

## Status

MitoQ is **not yet a proven sarcopenia therapy** in aged populations, but its demonstrated reduction of oxidative-stress damage supports continued exploration.
