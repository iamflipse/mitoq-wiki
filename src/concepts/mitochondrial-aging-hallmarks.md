# Mitochondrial Aging Hallmarks & ROS Generation

*Source: [§2 of the paper](../sources/mitoq-fulltext.md)*

Aging mitochondria show a self-amplifying damage cycle. The paper identifies four age-related mitochondrial alterations that generate ROS:

## ETC dysfunction

- The ETC (complexes I–IV + ATP synthase) is the primary source of both ATP and "incidental" ROS (superoxide at complexes I and III).
- With age: **ROS impairs complex I** by altering the redox state of iron-sulfur clusters (↓ electron transfer efficiency, ↓ catalytic activity).
- ROS **peroxidizes cardiolipin** — a phospholipid essential for inner-membrane organization and respiratory-complex stability; cardiolipin damage destabilizes complex assembly.
- **Oxidative damage to mtDNA** reduces expression/assembly of mitochondrially encoded respiratory subunits.
- Result: a **feed-forward cycle** — ROS-induced ETC dysfunction amplifies mtROS production → ↓ATP, partial dissipation of membrane potential, disrupted redox balance → impairs energy-dependent processes (fusion/fission, Ca²⁺ uptake) and activates redox-sensitive inflammatory pathways.

## Altered mitochondrial dynamics

- **Fusion** (OPA1 inner membrane; MFN1/MFN2 outer membrane) exchanges proteins/mtDNA; **fission** (FIS1, DRP1) fragments damaged mitochondria for clearance.
- In aged skeletal muscle: **↓OPA1** (sarcopenia link; OPA1 knockout mice show premature-aging phenotype); **OMa1** (oxidative-stress-activated inner-membrane protease) cleaves long OPA1 into short inactive fragments; **↓MFN2** in aged muscle disrupts metabolic homeostasis.
- ROS modifies fission regulators: aberrant **DRP1** phosphorylation/recruitment, ↓FIS1 expression/function.
- Aging models show **hyperfused, elongated mitochondrial networks** with inhibited fission → sustained mtROS production.

## Calcium dysregulation

- Physiological Ca²⁺ handling: ER stores Ca²⁺; IP3R–GRP75–VDAC channeling into mitochondria via MCU; efflux via **NCLX/HCX**.
- Excess ROS **oxidizes redox-sensitive residues** in IP3R, VDAC, and MCU components → disrupted ER→mitochondria Ca²⁺ transfer → progressive intramitochondrial Ca²⁺ accumulation.
- Excess matrix Ca²⁺ increases **mPTP** (mitochondrial permeability transition pore) sensitivity; sustained mPTP opening collapses membrane potential → impairs OXPHOS, increases electron leak and ROS.
- **mPTP Ca²⁺ vulnerability increases with age** — key aging hallmark of mitochondria.

## Mitochondrial ROS → inflammaging (NLRP3)

- Immune impairment + chronic low-grade inflammation (**inflammaging**) is a hallmark of aging; the **NLRP3 inflammasome** is the central regulator.
- NLRP3 activation is two-step: **priming** (PAMPs/DAMPs → TLRs → NF-κB transcription of NLRP3) + **activation** (membrane damage, K⁺ efflux, mitochondrial dysfunction → NLRP3–ASC–caspase-1 assembly → IL-1β/IL-18 maturation, GSDMD pyroptosis).
- **mtROS is an activator of NLRP3 priming**: mtROS enhances TLR4–MyD88 signaling → NLRP3 deubiquitination; rotenone-induced mtROS ↑NLRP3 activation; **mitochondrial-targeted antioxidants significantly reduce NLRP3 activation** — mtROS acts as a signaling mediator, not a passive byproduct.
- The resulting inflammatory loop (GSDMD pores releasing DAMPs) sustains chronic inflammation.
